Footwear with Ethnic Wear: Jutti or Sandals?

Updated November 2026

The wrong footwear can spoil an ethnic look, and your feet hurt if you wear it for long. This guide keeps both comfort and style in mind.

Which outfit, which footwear

  • Kurta set or anarkali: Jutti or flats.
  • Saree: Block heels or wedges, which also help the length look right.
  • Lehenga or sharara: Jutti or heels. The lehenga's length hides most of the footwear.
  • Everyday kurti: Kolhapuri chappals or flats.

About juttis

New juttis can feel a bit tight at first. Wear them at home for a few hours before the function so you don't get blisters.

Size and material

  • Synthetic material: It usually doesn't stretch, so get the right size straight away.
  • Between two sizes: Take the bigger size and add a soft insole to the pair.
  • Long functions: Carry an extra pair of flats.

Common mistakes

  • Wearing new juttis straight to a function.
  • Buying heels so high that you can't stand for long.
  • Ignoring the outfit's colour when choosing footwear. Neutral or gold-tone goes with most outfits.

Frequently asked questions

Which footwear goes with a lehenga?

Jutti or heels, since the lehenga's length hides most of the footwear.

What if my jutti feel tight?

Wear them at home for short spells over a few days to soften them, or take one size bigger.

What should I take for a long function?

Comfortable flats or block heels, and carry an extra pair.
