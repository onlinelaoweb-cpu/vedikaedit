How to Measure Yourself for Online Clothes

Updated November 2026

Most online size problems come from guessing. Five minutes with a measuring tape gives you numbers you can compare with any brand's size chart.

What you need

A soft measuring tape and a mirror. Wear thin, close-fitting clothes. Stand straight and breathe normally.

The 4 measurements

  • Chest: Measure around the fullest part of your chest, keeping the tape level across your back.
  • Waist: Measure around the narrowest part of your waist, usually just above the navel.
  • Hip: Measure around the fullest part of your hips, with your feet together.
  • Length: For kurtis, measure from the top of the shoulder down to where you want the hem to end.

Using the size chart

  • Compare numbers: Match your chest, waist and hip with the chart, not just S, M or L.
  • Body or garment size: Some charts list body measurements and others list the garment itself. A garment measurement should be a little bigger than you.
  • Between two sizes: Take the bigger size for cotton or a fitted cut, and the smaller one only if the fabric stretches.

A quick shortcut

Measure a kurti you already own and love. Lay it flat, measure across the chest (double it if the listing gives the full circumference) and compare it with the garment size on the listing.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring over thick clothes.
  • Pulling the tape too tight.
  • Using old measurements without checking them again.

Frequently asked questions

Which measurement matters most?

For tops and kurtis, chest and hip. For bottoms, waist and hip.

Should I check the garment size or my body size?

Both. Your body measurements tell you your size, and the garment measurements on the listing tell you how loose or fitted it will be.

How often should I measure again?

Every few months, or whenever your fit changes.
