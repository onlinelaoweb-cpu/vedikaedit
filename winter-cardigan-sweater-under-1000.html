Winter Cardigan and Sweater Under ₹1,000: What to Check

Updated November 2026

A good cardigan is the most used winter piece: it goes over kurtis, tops and dresses. Under ₹1,000 you can find warm ones, but the label on the listing needs careful reading.

Here is what to check so the sweater is warm, doesn't itch and doesn't lose shape after the first wash.

Material: what the label means

  • Wool blend: Warmer and softer, but needs gentle washing. Check the percentage of wool.
  • Acrylic: Light and cheap, easy to wash, and gives decent warmth. Cheaper ones can pill (form small balls) after a few washes.
  • Cotton knit: Less warm but good for mild winters and sensitive skin.
  • Fleece: Very warm and soft, good for very cold mornings, but not breathable for long hours indoors.

Fit and style

  • Length: Long cardigans cover kurtis and add a layered look. Short ones go with jeans and tops.
  • Size: Pick your normal size if you want a close fit, or one size up for room to layer a thermal inside.
  • Closure: Button-front is flexible for temperature changes. An open-front shrug is easiest to pair with ethnic wear.
  • Colour: Black, grey, camel and navy go with almost everything.

Care

Wash gently, in cold water, and dry flat or in shade. Hanging a wet sweater stretches it out. Most pilling happens from friction, so avoid wearing it under a heavy bag strap every day.

Common mistakes

  • Buying by photo without reading the material. 'Soft touch' on a listing says nothing about the fibre.
  • Skipping the size chart and then finding the sleeves too short.
  • Washing in hot water or wringing it out, which shrinks and stretches the sweater.

Frequently asked questions

Is a ₹1,000 cardigan warm enough?

For mild to moderate winters, yes. In very cold weather, wear a thermal underneath instead of buying a thicker, heavier sweater.

Wool or acrylic?

Wool blend is warmer and softer. Acrylic is cheaper and easier to wash. Check reviews for pilling either way.

How do I stop a sweater from itching?

Wear a thin cotton or thermal layer underneath, and look for 'soft' reviews that mention the neck and wrist area.
