Wedding Kurta Set Under ₹2,000: How to Choose

Updated November 2026

Going to a wedding as a guest but don't want to spend a lot on your outfit? You can get a good kurta set for ₹2,000, as long as you check a few things carefully.

Below is the checklist that avoids most size and quality mistakes if you read it before ordering.

Check these 5 things before you order

  • Fabric: Georgette, silk blend or chanderi-look suit party wear. Cotton is fine for daytime functions. Very shiny cheap polyester feels sticky in summer.
  • What's in the set: Read the listing description to see whether the kurta, bottom and dupatta all come together. In many listings the dupatta is sold separately.
  • Size chart: Measure a kurta that fits you well (chest, waist, length) and compare it with the listing's size chart. Don't rely on S, M, L alone; sizes vary by brand.
  • Work (embroidery): Light embroidery or print gives better quality at ₹2,000. Heavy zari work often looks weak at this budget.
  • Return policy: Check the return or exchange window before ordering, so a wrong size doesn't become a problem.

How to pick colours

Pastels (peach, mint, powder blue) look good for daytime functions. Deep colours (maroon, bottle green, royal blue) stand out more at evening or night functions.

Guests usually avoid the bride's colours, red and white. Your family may have its own tradition, so it's fine to ask once.

Common mistakes

  • Buying from the model's photo alone. Check the model's height and size in the listing.
  • Not reading whether the dupatta is included.
  • Ordering only 2-3 days before the wedding. If there's a delivery or size problem, there's no time left. Order at least a week ahead.

Frequently asked questions

Can you get a good kurta set for ₹2,000?

Yes. Choose by fabric, what's included in the set and reviews (especially about fit). If reviews keep complaining about size, skip that set.

How early should I order for a wedding?

At least a week ahead, so there's time to exchange if the size is wrong.

Do I need to buy jewellery separately?

No. With a heavy outfit, small jhumkas are enough. With a simple outfit, bigger jewellery completes the look.
